# Ear-to-Guitar Practice Plan (30 minutes a day)

**Goal:** Play the melodies you hear in your head on guitar, quickly and without mistakes.
Later, recognize chords by their function in the key (I, IV, V, vi and so on).

## Where you're stuck

You already hear in movable do. Two things slow you down:

1. **Finding the tonic.** Right now you work it out by reasoning, which takes 30–60 seconds.
   It needs to become something you feel right away.
2. **Getting from a syllable to a fret.** Even when you hear "mi re do," you still have to
   work out where that is on the neck. That gap, plus searching for notes, causes most mistakes.

So the work is making **tonic → solfege → fret** automatic.

## Rules for every session

- **Sing it before you play it.** If you can't sing the phrase, you can't hear it yet,
  and the guitar won't fix that.
- **Name it, play it, then check.** Name the syllable, play one note, and correct only if
  it's wrong. Don't slide around looking for notes. Searching trains you to search.
- **Slow and accurate first.** Speed comes later. A wrong note you play quickly teaches you the wrong note.
- **Keep one minor system.** Use la-based or do-based minor, whichever you already use, and don't switch.

## Daily 30 minutes

| Block | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Tonic and scale degrees** | 7 min | **Functional Ear Trainer** (free app by Alain Benbassat): it plays a cadence and one note, and you name the degree. Then **tonic drills**: put on a random song, set a 10-second timer, and hum a long steady note until it fits under everything, especially at phrase endings and the end of the chorus. That note is do (or la). Check it on the guitar. |
| **2. Solfege on the fretboard** | 8 min | Learn the major scale **by syllable** in about 3 positions: do on the 6th string, the 5th string and the 4th string. Change key every day. Sing each syllable as you play it. Then drill: think of a syllable and play it instantly within the current position. |
| **3. Play back melodies** | 15 min | Short phrases → play them back → real songs (see the phases). Always sing the phrase first. Count how many tries each phrase takes. |

### Tonic tips

- Ignore the first note of the song. Melodies often start on sol or mi.
- The tonic is where the music **rests**: phrase endings, the end of the chorus, the final bass note.
- **Humming test:** hold one steady hummed note over the song. The tonic fits almost everywhere.
  A wrong guess clashes within a bar or two.
- If home sounds sad or dark, the song is probably minor, so the home note is la (in la-based minor).

## Weekly test (5 min, Sunday)

Write both numbers down every week. They're how you'll see real progress.

1. **Tonic speed:** time yourself finding the tonic on 5 songs you haven't heard before. Record the average in seconds.
2. **Playback accuracy:** play back 10 random 4-note phrases. Record how many you got right on the first try.

## Phases and milestones

### Phase 1: Weeks 1–4, the basics
- Functional Ear Trainer: diatonic notes at 90% or better, answered without thinking.
- You can play every syllable in any key in at least 2 positions.
- Play back 3–4 note diatonic phrases (from an app, or nursery rhymes and folk tunes).
- ✅ **Milestone:** tonic in **under 10 seconds** on most pop songs.

### Phase 2: Months 2–3, real melodies
- Pick out real songs by ear: folk tunes, hymns and Christmas songs first, then simple pop vocal lines.
- Phrases grow to 5–8 notes, including leaps (do→sol, la→re).
- **Sing along while you play** (in unison) on melodies you already know. This welds the voice and the hands together.
- ✅ **Milestone:** first try correct on 70% or more of 4–6 note diatonic phrases. Tonic in **under 5 seconds**.

### Phase 3: Months 3–6, chromatic notes and the first chords
- Add the most common chromatic notes: **te** (♭7), **fi** (♯4), **si** (raised 7 in minor), **me/le**.
- Chords, part 1: hear the **bass note** in solfege first, then the quality (major or minor).
  Start with I, IV, V and vi in real songs.
- ✅ **Milestone:** after two or three listens, you can play the melody of a typical pop chorus
  with no searching. You can name I–IV–V–vi progressions in songs.

### Phase 4: Months 6–12, hearing in real time
- Play what you imagine in real time, the way you'd improvise a melody you hear in your head.
- Chords: add ii and iii, the common borrowed chords (♭VII, iv, ♭VI) and V/V.
- ✅ **Milestone:** what you hear in your head comes out of the guitar with few mistakes.

### Realistic timeline
- **Tonic speed:** 4–8 weeks once you stop reasoning it out and use the humming test.
- **Big noticeable change:** about 3 months.
- **Playing what you hear, for most melodies, without mistakes:** about 9–12 months of steady daily practice.

## Tools

- **Functional Ear Trainer**: the core tool for tonic and degrees.
- **EarMaster** (paid): melodic dictation and singing exercises with a microphone.
- **toneDear.com** / **Perfect Ear**: extra interval and melody drills.
- **Rove EarTrainer** (this app): plays a cadence and a melody for you to play back on guitar
  (block 3), times how fast you find the tonic (block 1 and the weekly test), and drills chords
  by function. Use hands-free mode to keep both hands on the guitar.
